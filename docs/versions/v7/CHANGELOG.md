## How V7 differs from V6

- **Unchanged:** the simulator (V0–V5) and the validation tooling (V6). All earlier tests pass unchanged.
- **New:** an `rl/` package with three distinct parts:
  1. **The RL interface** (`rl/env.py`): environment, observation, action, reward, termination. This is the deliverable other work builds on.
  2. **Non-learning baselines and evaluation** (`rl/policies.py`, `rl/evaluate.py`): fixed comparators and the measurement harness.
  3. **A learning experiment** (`rl/experiment.py`, `rl/reports/experiment_report.md`): a cross-entropy-method tuning of the rule-based policy's 4 thresholds. It is a demonstration that learning from episodes works through the interface. It is **not** a claim of a good strategy.

## Dependencies

**None added.** `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` are unchanged. Gymnasium and numpy are deliberately not used; the reasons are in IMPLEMENTATION.md §2. The signatures follow Gymnasium's conventions.

## A design change made during V7 (documented, not hidden)

The first version made the decision **just after** each line crossing, for the end of the **next** lap. Tracing seed 16 for CODE_FLOW showed that a Safety Car starting mid-lap was never visible at a decision. Under V5 a Safety Car ends exactly as the leader crosses the line, and a car running alone is the leader. The decision now comes **300 m before the line** and applies to that line. That made mid-lap flags visible, and a stop's cost now lands in the same step as the decision. The first experiment run, with the old timing, was stopped and discarded. All results in this version use the final timing.

## Results

### The interface (verified by tests)

The interface does what is documented; see the test table.

### Experimental result (one experiment, 20 held-out episodes; details in IMPLEMENTATION.md §7)

| Policy | Mean race time (s) ± std | Paired vs rule-based (s) | Better / worse |
|---|---|---|---|
| stay out | 5233.3 ± 130.3 | +40.7 ± 13.9 | 0 / 20 |
| fixed one-stop | 5196.8 ± 129.1 | +4.2 ± 12.7 | 2 / 18 |
| rule-based (hand-set) | 5192.6 ± 119.7 | — | — |
| random (10 % pit) | 5356.5 ± 134.4 | +163.9 ± 108.9 | 0 / 20 |
| tuned rule-based (CEM) | 5192.8 ± 118.3 | +0.2 ± 5.4 | 4 / 13 |

- **Finding (this simulator only):** the hand-written, weather- and flag-aware rule beats a fixed one-stop and the trivial baselines on most held-out races.
- **Negative finding:** CEM tuning on 3 training races did **not** improve on the hand-set rule. None of those 3 races had a VSC or Safety Car, so the tuned thresholds overfitted.
- **Not claimed:** that any agent learned a good strategy, or anything about real F1. The tyre, fuel and race-control parameters are unvalidated placeholders (V6).

## Known limitations

See IMPLEMENTATION.md §9 and §8 (V6 limits). In short:

- about 2 s per episode;
- one controlled car, pit decisions only;
- the reward is time, not position;
- one call per lap;
- tyre wear is observed exactly;
- opponents follow fixed plans;
- the scenarios are simple;
- one small experiment;
- learned behaviour reflects the model's placeholder parameters.
