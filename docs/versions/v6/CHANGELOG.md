## How V6 differs from V5

V6 changes **no simulator behaviour**. All V0–V5 code and results are identical, and every earlier regression test passes unchanged. V6 adds a separate evaluation layer: real reference data, tooling, configurations, a generated report, and docs. The calibrated parameter values are **not** adopted by the simulator.

## Dependencies

- **None added.** `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` are unchanged.
- The fetch script uses the standard library's `urllib`. The rest uses `json`, `re`, `statistics` and `pathlib`.
- **Network** is needed only by `python3 -m validation.fetch_reference`. Everything else, including all tests, runs offline.

## Findings, sorted by strength of evidence

### Validated against real data

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Baseline flying lap is 14.7–16.2 s (18.5–20.5 %) slower than each team's best Monza qualifying lap, 2024 and 2025 | report, qualifying tables |
| Modelled Monza is 107 m (1.85 %) too long | 2024 average speed × time = 5 793.0 m |
| Baseline `PIT_LOSS` (22 s) is 3.4–3.6 s below the measured median (25.62 s in 2024, 25.36 s in 2025) | 14 + 8 measured stops |
| One fitted effective grip (2.9358, from 2024) predicts 2025 qualifying with MAE 0.52 s and bias +0.52 s, but gets the fastest team wrong | held-out comparison |
| Calibrated pit loss (25.62 s) is within 0.26 s of the 2025 median | held-out comparison |
| With both fitted values, a single model car's race is 2.1 % (2025) and 2.6 % (2024) faster than the winner | race table |

### Unverified assumptions (no data in V6 can test them)

- Tyre compound grips, wear and wet behaviour.
- The fuel-burn rate and the fuel-mass effect.
- Traffic, dirty air, DRS and overtaking values.
- Weather rates.
- Race-control effects.
- Individual car parameters (power, drag, downforce) and corner radii.
- The medium → hard compounds assumed in the race check.

### Interpretations, not established

- That the race-time gap comes mainly from under-modelled race-vs-qualifying pace (fuel and tyres) rather than from the missing standing start and traffic.
- That the 2.94 effective grip compensates mostly for geometry rather than for the aerodynamic or power values.

### Synthetic demonstrations

- Only in `test_validation.py`: small invented files and lap lists, labelled `SYNTHETIC`, used to test error handling and arithmetic.
- No reported number uses synthetic data.

**Mutation check (manual, not committed).** In a scratch copy, 13 deliberate bugs were introduced into the tooling one at a time:

- the seconds range check removed;
- kph multiplied instead of divided;
- Q3 only;
- no exclusion of disturbed laps;
- no 107 % filter;
- the speed-count minimum removed;
- MAE without absolute values;
- MAPE without the ×100;
- mean instead of median for pit loss;
- the bisection direction flipped;
- no grip scaling in races;
- no config validation;
- multiple races accepted.

The first run missed two of them (the speed-count minimum and multiple races accepted). Two synthetic files were added; all 13 are now caught.

## Deliberately absent in V6

- No model changes to reduce the errors found. The task was to measure them.
- No new data beyond Monza 2024 and 2025.
- No telemetry (FastF1 or similar would be a new dependency).
- No traffic or overtaking validation from per-lap positions.
- No tyre or fuel calibration, because there is no compound or fuel data.
- RL interface: V7.

## Known limitations

See IMPLEMENTATION.md §11. In short:

- 2 races, 1 track, 4 teams;
- a held-out test across years only;
- imperfect reference equivalents (driver differences in qualifying, standing start and traffic in races);
- a volunteer data source with no correctness guarantee;
- a non-commercial data licence;
- calibrated values that are effective, not physical, and not adopted by the simulator.
